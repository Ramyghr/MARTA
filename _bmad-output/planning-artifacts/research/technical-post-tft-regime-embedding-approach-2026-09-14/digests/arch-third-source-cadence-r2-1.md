# Digest: Third source for gating-vs-concatenation pattern + cadence mismatch (round 2)

## Findings

- **Claim:** In contextual RL under discontinuous context/regime shifts, directly concatenating the context signal into the policy's inputs is a strictly weaker representational/empirical strategy than using it to multiplicatively modulate the network (via a hypernetwork/shared-adapter mechanism) — concatenation "must realize [discontinuous] structure only indirectly," while modulation "can exactly represent certain functions outside the concatenation hypothesis class," yielding an 11.5% average improvement in zero-shot generalization (0.80 vs 0.74 AER on non-overlapping/OOD contexts).
  **Source:** "Dynamics-Aligned Shared Hypernetworks for Contextual RL under Discontinuous Shifts," arXiv:2602.06550v2 — **Publisher:** Hamburg University of Technology / Univ. Tübingen / Santa Fe Institute — **Published:** 2026-05-11 — **Accessed:** 2026-09-14 — **Confidence:** medium-high (primary source, read directly, empirical benchmark numbers quoted) — **Class:** pattern — **a genuinely THIRD, independent source (general RL robotics benchmarks — CartPole, Cheetah, Reacher — no citation link to the two prior trading-specific sources), generalizing the pattern from "regime signal in trading" to the broader class of "discontinuous context/regime shifts in RL." Together with round 1's two trading-specific papers (arXiv 2604.10996, arXiv 2608.12251), this now meets a reasonable two/three-source bar across independent domains for: "concatenating a regime/context signal directly into model inputs is less stable/robust than mediating it through a gating or modulation mechanism."**

- **Claim (caveat/complication):** Adding a mixture-of-experts routing/gating layer conditioned on regime identity to state-space dynamics models does NOT reliably help — "operator-level mixture models consistently fail to outperform a single-expert baseline. Increasing the number of experts leads to inverse scaling," and even oracle (ground-truth) regime routing doesn't prevent degradation, because soft routing induces temporal smoothing that destroys the underlying attractor geometry.
  **Source:** "More Experts, Worse Dynamics: Inverse Scaling and Spectral Bias in Mixture-of-Experts State-Space Models," arXiv:2608.21840 — **Publisher:** independent author — **Published:** 2026 — **Accessed:** 2026-09-14 — **Confidence:** low-medium (single-author paper, synthetic benchmarks only — Mackey-Glass chaotic dynamics, not real-world/financial; does not directly compare gating vs. concatenation, only MoE routing granularity) — **Class:** failure-claim — **Important complication: gating-based regime conditioning is NOT automatically safe either — this failure mode can persist inside the gating approach itself. Do not over-claim "gating always fixes it."**

- **Claim:** In LLM-agent trading pipelines, combining signals that update at different rates (continuous market/price data vs. sporadic news/text) creates a documented synchronization risk: "pairing the latest available news item with the current price can... misrepresent the true decision context when the text signal is stale." Proposes "Time-Aware Retrieval" (decay terms prioritizing recent regimes) and an "Outcome Embargo" principle (episodes can't expose outcomes until current time exceeds outcome-realization time) as mitigations.
  **Source:** "Agentic Trading: When LLM Agents Meet Financial Markets," arXiv:2605.19337v1 (submitted to Expert Systems with Applications) — **Publisher:** Shenzhen University — **Published:** 2026-05-19 — **Accessed:** 2026-09-14 — **Confidence:** medium (primary source, read directly, but a survey/design-pattern paper, not an empirical ablation quantifying staleness cost) — **Class:** pattern — **best available source for the cadence-mismatch question: names the failure mode explicitly and proposes concrete mitigations (recency decay, embargo/no-leakage rule), directly mapping onto "a fast layer reading a slow layer's stale signal."**

## What was looked for and could not be found
- A genuine non-academic, production/engineering account (blog, talk, post-mortem) from an actual trading firm about sharing one regime/state signal across components with different update frequencies. Every search returned either academic papers or marketing/SEO content from quant-adjacent content farms showing clear source-quality red flags (speculative language, no named authors/firms) — deliberately excluded rather than cited. Real gap remains open.
- A study directly quantifying "training instability" (literal divergence/loss-curve sense) from concatenating a regime signal, as opposed to degraded downstream performance/generalization — the third source found demonstrates a generalization/accuracy gap (11.5%), not a training-instability curve; only a partial match to round 1's instability-framed findings, not an exact-mechanism match.
- Any source discussing cadence mismatch specifically between an hourly and a weekly (or comparably coarse) decision layer sharing one signal, as opposed to the finer-grained "continuous price feed vs. sporadic news" mismatch the Agentic Trading survey covers — the exact hourly-vs-weekly framing was not found in any source.
